# Bottleneck types

A system moves only as fast as its most constrained step. Work piles up in front of it. Speed up that step and the whole business moves. Speed up anything else and little happens.

Constraints are not only about speed. Classify the bottleneck as one of six types, because the type decides the route and the kind of fix.

| Type | Recognize it by | Measure | Route |
|------|-----------------|---------|-------|
| Customer acquisition | Too little visibility, too few leads, leads not converting, slow replies to leads, few repeat sales | Leads per week, reply time, conversion per stage, repeat rate | Market This (or basic help here) |
| Throughput and waiting | Work piles up, long lead times, many handoffs, approvals that stall | Units per week per step, queue size, lead time versus work time | Consult Anything |
| Quality | Errors, rework, complaints, returns | Rework or error percentage, complaints per 100 orders | Consult Anything |
| Information | Data missing when needed, reports take days, numbers do not match, decisions on gut feel | Hours spent on reporting, delay between event and insight | Consult Anything |
| Decisions and policy | Everything waits for the owner, rules nobody questions anymore, unclear mandates | Items waiting for a decision, average decision delay | Consult Anything; escalate if there is resistance |
| People, roles and strategy | Knowledge sits with one person, unclear roles, staff shortage, burnout, direction unclear | Single points of failure, open vacancies, overtime | Escalation or referral; prepare what you can |

## Finding the constraint

1. Put the throughput per week per step side by side. The lowest sustained capacity with a queue in front of it is the prime candidate.
2. Check flow efficiency per step: work time divided by lead time. Very low efficiency points to waiting, approvals or handoffs.
3. Check rework: a step with high rework effectively has lower capacity than it seems.
4. Check where money leaks. A step that loses paying customers outranks a step that is merely slow.
5. Policy check: ask "who decided it has to work this way?" The answer often reveals a policy constraint.

## Common traps

- The loudest problem is not always the constraint. Test it against the numbers.
- The owner is often the constraint. Name it kindly and concretely: "Seven decisions per week wait for you, on average for three days."
- Lots of leads but slow replies: the constraint is the reply step, not marketing. More ads will not help.
- Few leads but everything else idle: the constraint is customer acquisition.
- Staff shortage is a capacity constraint. First check how much of the existing capacity is lost to rework, waiting and admin before concluding they need more people.

## Gate 2
